## The Key Idea

Letters and sounds are not the same thing. When you read a word, count the **letters**. When you say a word, count the **sounds**. Those two numbers often disagree.

- **More letters than sounds** — some letters are silent, or two letters share one sound.
- **More sounds than letters** — one letter produces two sounds.

---

## 1. More Letters Than Sounds

### Silent Letters

A silent letter is written but produces no sound. It adds to the letter count without adding to the sound count.

| Word   | Letters | Sounds | IPA      | Silent Letter(s) |
| ------ | ------- | ------ | -------- | ---------------- |
| knife  | 5       | 3      | /naɪf/   | k                |
| knight | 6       | 3      | /naɪt/   | k, gh            |
| write  | 5       | 3      | /raɪt/   | w                |
| wrap   | 4       | 3      | /ræp/    | w                |
| lamb   | 4       | 3      | /læm/    | b                |
| debt   | 4       | 3      | /dɛt/    | b                |
| castle | 6       | 4      | /ˈkæsəl/ | t                |
| listen | 6       | 5      | /ˈlɪsən/ | t                |
| hour   | 4       | 3      | /aʊɚ/    | h                |

### Digraphs (Two Letters → One Sound)

A digraph is a pair of letters that together represent a single sound. The letter count is 2, the sound count is 1.

| Letters | Sound    | Phoneme | Example | IPA    |
| ------- | -------- | ------- | ------- | ------ |
| sh      | sh-sound | /ʃ/     | ship    | /ʃɪp/  |
| ch      | ch-sound | /tʃ/    | chip    | /tʃɪp/ |
| th      | th-sound | /θ/     | think   | /θɪŋk/ |
| ph      | f-sound  | /f/     | phone   | /foʊn/ |
| ng      | ng-sound | /ŋ/     | ring    | /rɪŋ/  |
| ck      | k-sound  | /k/     | back    | /bæk/  |

#### Example: _ship_

> s – h – i – p → **4 letters**
> /ʃ/ – /ɪ/ – /p/ → **3 sounds**

The digraph _sh_ is 2 letters that produce 1 sound, so the letter count is higher.

### Double Letters (Two Letters → One Sound)

When the same letter appears twice in a row, it almost always represents a single sound.

| Word   | Letters | Sounds | IPA     |
| ------ | ------- | ------ | ------- |
| bell   | 4       | 3      | /bɛl/   |
| butter | 6       | 4      | /ˈbʌtɚ/ |
| glass  | 5       | 4      | /ɡlæs/  |
| egg    | 3       | 2      | /ɛɡ/    |
| happy  | 5       | 4      | /ˈhæpi/ |

## 2. More Sounds Than Letters

### The Letter X

The letter _X_ is the most common case of one letter producing two sounds. Depending on position, it maps to either /ks/ or /ɡz/.

#### X = /ks/ (at the end of a word, or before a consonant)

| Word | Letters | Sounds | IPA     |
| ---- | ------- | ------ | ------- |
| fox  | 3       | 4      | /fɑks/  |
| box  | 3       | 4      | /bɑks/  |
| mix  | 3       | 4      | /mɪks/  |
| next | 4       | 5      | /nɛkst/ |

#### X = /ɡz/ (between vowels, before a stressed vowel)

| Word  | Letters | Sounds | IPA       |
| ----- | ------- | ------ | --------- |
| exam  | 4       | 5      | /ɪɡˈzæm/  |
| exact | 5       | 6      | /ɪɡˈzækt/ |
| exist | 5       | 6      | /ɪɡˈzɪst/ |

#### Example: _fox_

> f – o – x → **3 letters**
> /f/ – /ɑ/ – /k/ – /s/ → **4 sounds**

The letter _x_ accounts for 1 letter but 2 sounds (/k/ + /s/), so the sound count is higher.

### The "Cute U" — Long U after certain consonants

When _u_ follows _c_, _f_, _m_, _p_, or _h_ and is in a stressed syllable, it is pronounced /juː/ — two sounds (/j/ + /uː/) from one letter.

| Word | Letters | Sounds | IPA      |
| ---- | ------- | ------ | -------- |
| cute | 4       | 4      | /kjuːt/  |
| cure | 4       | 4      | /kjʊɚ/   |
| mute | 4       | 4      | /mjuːt/  |
| fuse | 4       | 4      | /fjuːz/  |
| huge | 4       | 4      | /hjuːdʒ/ |

Here the letter count and sound count happen to balance because these words also contain a silent _e_. The silent _e_ removes one letter from the sound count, and the /j/ from the _u_ adds one sound back, leaving the totals even. But if you focus on the _u_ alone: **one letter, two sounds**.

## How to Count Sounds in a Word

1. Say the word slowly out loud.
2. Count each distinct sound — not each letter.
3. Use the IPA transcription to check your count.

| Word     | IPA       | Letter Count | Sound Count |
| -------- | --------- | ------------ | ----------- |
| through  | /θruː/    | 7            | 3           |
| strength | /strɛŋkθ/ | 8            | 7           |
| fox      | /fɑks/    | 3            | 4           |
| ship     | /ʃɪp/     | 4            | 3           |
| mix      | /mɪks/    | 3            | 4           |
| knight   | /naɪt/    | 6            | 3           |

The word _through_ has 7 letters but only 3 sounds — the letter-to-sound ratio is more than 2:1. The word _fox_ has 3 letters but 4 sounds. Neither is unusual in English; both patterns appear constantly.
