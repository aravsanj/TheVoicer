## Sound Length Is Not Fixed

In English, the same sound can be held for a longer or shorter time depending on where it appears and what comes after it. This variation in **length** is one of the things that gives English its characteristic rhythm.

## Vowel Length: Voiced vs. Voiceless Consonants

A vowel sound is held **longer** before a **voiced** consonant than before a **voiceless** consonant.

Compare these pairs. The vowel in each word is the same sound, but it's held for different lengths of time:

| Shorter vowel (before voiceless) | Longer vowel (before voiced) |
|----------------------------------|------------------------------|
| <Word id="cap" /> | <Word id="cab" /> |
| <Word id="bet" /> | <Word id="bed" /> |
| <Word id="rice" /> | <Word id="rise" /> |
| <Word id="back" /> | <Word id="bag" /> |
| <Word id="leaf" /> | <Word id="leave" /> |

The vowel in <Word id="cab" compact /> is noticeably longer than in <Word id="cap" compact />, even though both use the <Sound id="short-a" compact /> sound. Native speakers rely on this length difference, not just the final consonant, to distinguish the two words.

## Vowel Length: Sentence Position

A vowel sound is held **longer** when it ends a word that appears at the **end of a sentence**.

| Mid-sentence (shorter)              | End of sentence (longer)        |
|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| *I saw her **go** quickly.*         | *I watched her **go**.*         |
| *The answer is **no**, actually.*   | *The answer is **no**.*         |
| *She said **yes** right away.*      | *She finally said **yes**.*     |

This lengthening at the end of a sentence is connected to falling intonation. The voice slows and drops, which extends the final sound.

## Consonant Length: Final Position

Final consonant sounds follow their own length pattern.

**Voiceless final consonants** (<Sound id="f" compact />, <Sound id="s" compact />, <Sound id="sh" compact />, <Sound id="th" compact />, <Sound id="ch" compact />) are held **longer** than voiced final consonants (<Sound id="v" compact />, <Sound id="z" compact />, <Sound id="zh" compact />, <Sound id="dh" compact />, <Sound id="j" compact />).

| Shorter (voiced final)  | Longer (voiceless final) |
|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| <Word id="leave" /> | <Word id="leaf" /> |
| <Word id="prize" /> | <Word id="price" /> |
| <Word id="rouge" /> | <Word id="rush" /> |

This may seem counterintuitive. Voiced sounds typically involve more vocal activity. But the compensation works like a trade-off: the **vowel** before a voiced consonant gets longer, while the **voiced consonant itself** is shorter. The voiceless consonant, by contrast, gets more time and airflow.

## Stops Cannot Be Held

The length rules above apply to **continuant** sounds, meaning sounds you can sustain. **Stops** (sounds made by fully blocking and releasing the airflow) cannot be held.

| Voiceless stops | Voiced stops |
|-----------------|--------------|
| /p/ (<Word id="cap" compact />) | /b/ (<Word id="cab" compact />) |
| /t/ (<Word id="bet" compact />) | /d/ (<Word id="bed" compact />) |
| /k/ (<Word id="back" compact />) | /ɡ/ (<Word id="bag" compact />) |

For words ending in these sounds, the length difference is perceived through the **vowel** before the stop, not through the stop itself. <Word id="cap" compact /> and <Word id="cab" compact /> are distinguished primarily because the vowel in <Word id="cab" compact /> is longer, not because you hold the final <Sound id="b" compact /> longer than the <Sound id="p" compact />.
