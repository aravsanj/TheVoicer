## The Basic Unit of Speech

Every word you say is made up of small beats of sound. These beats are called **syllables**.

A syllable is a unit of pronunciation built around **one vowel sound**. You cannot have a syllable without a vowel sound at its center — consonants alone cannot form a syllable.

> **Key rule:** One vowel sound = one syllable.

---

## The Vowel Sound Is the Core

The vowel is the loudest, most resonant part of a syllable. Consonants attach to it before and after, but the vowel is what makes a syllable "ring."

| Word   | Vowel Sound | Syllables |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| sun    | /ʌ/         | 1         |
| open   | /oʊ/ + /ə/  | 2         |
| animal | /æ/ + /ɪ/ + /ə/ | 3     |

---

## Vowel Letters vs. Vowel Sounds

A common trap: **vowel letters** (a, e, i, o, u) are not the same as **vowel sounds**. Syllables are counted by *sounds*, not letters.

- **cake** → looks like 2 vowel letters (a, e), but the *e* is silent → **1 syllable**, one vowel sound /eɪ/
- **blue** → 2 vowel letters (u, e), but pronounced as one sound /u/ → **1 syllable**
- **fire** → 2 vowel letters, but pronounced as the diphthong /aɪ/ + syllabic /ɚ/ → **2 syllables** (in most American speech: *fi-yer*)

Always count sounds, not letters.

---

## Syllables Give Words Their Shape

Think of syllables as the **skeleton** of a word. They determine:

- How many "beats" a word has when spoken
- Where stress falls (louder, longer beats)
- The rhythm of a sentence

Mispronouncing syllable count is one of the most common reasons native speakers have trouble understanding learners. Getting the syllable count right is step one.

## Quick Check

How many syllables do you hear?

| Word        | Answer |
|-------------|--------|
| cat         | 1      |
| window      | 2      |
| beautiful   | 3      |
| education   | 4      |
| communication | 5    |

Say each word aloud and tap your finger for each beat. That's how many syllables it has.
