## 1. Your Speech Organs (Articulators)

Speech is produced by shaping and directing airflow from your lungs through a series of movable parts called **articulators**.

| Articulator | Role in Speech |
|-------------|----------------|
| **Lips** | Shape the opening; used for sounds like <Sound id="p" compact />, <Sound id="b" compact />, <Sound id="m" compact />, <Sound id="w" compact /> |
| **Teeth** | Work with the tongue tip; used for sounds like <Sound id="th" compact />, <Sound id="dh" compact />, <Sound id="f" compact />, <Sound id="v" compact /> |
| **Jaw** | Opens and closes to control mouth space; affects vowel height |
| **Tongue** | Most active articulator; tip, front, and back each target different sounds |
| **Roof of mouth** | Includes gum ridge, hard palate, and soft palate; the tongue targets each zone |
| **Nose** | Nasal cavity resonates for <Sound id="m" compact />, <Sound id="n" compact />, <Sound id="ng" compact /> |
| **Vocal cords** | Vibrate to produce voice; sit in the throat above the lungs |

### Tongue Zones

The tongue is divided into three active zones:

- **Tip**: touches the gum ridge for <Sound id="t" compact />, <Sound id="d" compact />, <Sound id="n" compact />, <Sound id="l" compact />
- **Front (blade)**: used for <Sound id="sh" compact />, <Sound id="zh" compact />, <Sound id="ch" compact />, <Sound id="j" compact />
- **Back**: rises toward the soft palate for <Sound id="k" compact />, <Sound id="g" compact />, <Sound id="ng" compact />

### Roof of Mouth Zones

- **Gum ridge (alveolar ridge)**: the bumpy ridge just behind your upper front teeth; used for <Sound id="t" compact />, <Sound id="d" compact />, <Sound id="s" compact />, <Sound id="z" compact />, <Sound id="n" compact />, <Sound id="l" compact />
- **Hard palate**: the firm bony roof further back; used for <Sound id="sh" compact />, <Sound id="zh" compact />, <Sound id="ch" compact />, <Sound id="j" compact />, <Sound id="y" compact />
- **Soft palate (velum)**: the flexible rear section; used for <Sound id="k" compact />, <Sound id="g" compact />, <Sound id="ng" compact />; lowers to allow nasal airflow for <Sound id="m" compact />, <Sound id="n" compact />, <Sound id="ng" compact />

### Tongue Zone × Roof of Mouth Zone

The three tongue zones (Tip, Front/blade, Back) always target a surface on the roof of the mouth. Some consonants bypass the tongue entirely and use the lips or glottis instead.

| Tongue Zone | Targets | Sounds |
|-------------|---------|--------|
| Tip | Teeth | <Sound id="th" compact /> (<Word id="think" compact />), <Sound id="dh" compact /> (<Word id="this" compact />) |
| Tip | Gum ridge | <Sound id="t" compact />, <Sound id="d" compact />, <Sound id="s" compact />, <Sound id="z" compact />, <Sound id="n" compact />, <Sound id="l" compact /> |
| Front (blade) | Hard palate | <Sound id="sh" compact /> (<Word id="shoe" compact />), <Sound id="zh" compact /> (<Word id="measure" compact />), <Sound id="ch" compact /> (<Word id="chair" compact />), <Sound id="j" compact /> (<Word id="jump" compact />), <Sound id="y" compact /> (<Word id="yes" compact />) |
| Back | Soft palate | <Sound id="k" compact /> (<Word id="cat" compact />), <Sound id="g" compact /> (<Word id="go" compact />), <Sound id="ng" compact /> (<Word id="sing" compact />) |
| **No tongue involved** | Lips | <Sound id="p" compact /> (<Word id="pen" compact />), <Sound id="b" compact /> (<Word id="bed" compact />), <Sound id="m" compact /> (<Word id="map" compact />), <Sound id="w" compact /> (<Word id="wet" compact />) |
| **No tongue involved** | Lower lip + upper teeth | <Sound id="f" compact /> (<Word id="fan" compact />), <Sound id="v" compact /> (<Word id="van" compact />) |
| **No tongue involved** | Glottis (throat) | <Sound id="h" compact /> (<Word id="hat" compact />) |

## 2. Voiced vs. Voiceless Sounds

Every sound is either **voiced** (vocal cords vibrate) or **voiceless** (vocal cords stay apart, only air passes through).

### How to Feel the Difference

Place the back of your hand against your throat and say these pairs slowly:

- **sss** (voiceless) → **zzz** (voiced): feel the buzz appear for <Sound id="z" compact />
- **fff** (voiceless) → **vvv** (voiced): feel the buzz appear for <Sound id="v" compact />

Alternatively, cover both ears with your hands. Voiced sounds create a hum you can hear from inside your head; voiceless sounds are silent internally.

## 4. Vowels: Always Voiced

All English vowels are **voiced**. There are no voiceless vowels in standard American English.

For vowels, **the tongue never contacts any surface.** It floats freely inside the mouth, shaping the resonance space by hovering at different heights and positions. For consonants, some articulator always makes contact or causes constriction (tongue, lips, or glottis). That's the key difference.

Vowels are shaped by:

1. **Tongue body position (front/back)**: how far forward or back the tongue body sits
2. **Tongue height**: how close the tongue is to the roof of the mouth; high = close to roof, low = far from roof
3. **Lip rounding**: whether the lips round (as in <Sound id="long-u" compact />) or spread (as in <Sound id="long-e" compact />)

| Tongue Body Position | Height | Lip Rounding | Sounds | Examples |
|----------------------|--------|-------------|--------|---------|
| Front | High | Unrounded | <Sound id="long-e" compact />, <Sound id="short-i" compact /> | <Word id="eat" compact />, <Word id="sit" compact /> |
| Front | Mid | Unrounded | <Sound id="long-a" compact />, <Sound id="short-e" compact /> | <Word id="face" compact />, <Word id="bet" compact /> |
| Front | Low | Unrounded | <Sound id="short-a" compact /> | <Word id="cat" compact /> |
| Central | Mid | Unrounded | <Sound id="schwa" compact />, <Sound id="short-u" compact /> | <Word id="about" compact />, <Word id="cup" compact /> |
| Central | Mid (r-colored) | Unrounded | <Sound id="r-vowel" compact />, <Sound id="r-schwa" compact /> | <Word id="bird" compact />, <Word id="butter" compact /> |
| Back | High | Rounded | <Sound id="long-u" compact />, <Sound id="short-oo" compact /> | <Word id="food" compact />, <Word id="book" compact /> |
| Back | Mid | Rounded | <Sound id="long-o" compact />, <Sound id="aw" compact /> | <Word id="home" compact />, <Word id="law" compact /> |
| Back | Low | Unrounded | <Sound id="short-o" compact /> | <Word id="father" compact /> |

Because all vowels involve continuous voicing and an open vocal tract, they carry the most volume and sustain the longest in connected speech.
