## 1. Your Speech Organs (Articulators)

Speech is produced by shaping and directing airflow from your lungs through a series of movable parts called **articulators**.

| Articulator | Role in Speech |
|-------------|----------------|
| **Lips** | Shape the opening; used for sounds like /p/, /b/, /m/, /w/ |
| **Teeth** | Work with the tongue tip; used for sounds like /θ/, /ð/, /f/, /v/ |
| **Jaw** | Opens and closes to control mouth space; affects vowel height |
| **Tongue** | Most active articulator — tip, front, back all used for different sounds |
| **Roof of mouth** | Includes gum ridge, hard palate, and soft palate — tongue targets these |
| **Nose** | Nasal cavity resonates for /m/, /n/, /ŋ/ |
| **Vocal cords** | Vibrate to produce voice; sit in the throat above the lungs |

### Tongue Zones

The tongue is divided into three active zones:

- **Tip** — touches the gum ridge for /t/, /d/, /n/, /l/
- **Front (blade)** — used for /ʃ/, /ʒ/, /tʃ/, /dʒ/
- **Back** — rises toward the soft palate for /k/, /g/, /ŋ/

### Roof of Mouth Zones

- **Gum ridge (alveolar ridge)** — the bumpy ridge just behind your upper front teeth; used for /t/, /d/, /s/, /z/, /n/, /l/
- **Hard palate** — the firm bony roof further back; used for /ʃ/, /ʒ/, /tʃ/, /dʒ/, /j/
- **Soft palate (velum)** — the flexible rear section; used for /k/, /g/, /ŋ/; lowers to allow nasal airflow for /m/, /n/, /ŋ/

### Tongue Zone × Roof of Mouth Zone

The three tongue zones (Tip, Front/blade, Back) always target a surface on the roof of the mouth. Some consonants bypass the tongue entirely and use the lips or glottis instead.

| Tongue Zone | Targets | Sounds |
|-------------|---------|--------|
| Tip | Teeth | /θ/ (*think*), /ð/ (*this*) |
| Tip | Gum ridge | /t/, /d/, /s/, /z/, /n/, /l/ |
| Front (blade) | Hard palate | /ʃ/ (*shoe*), /ʒ/ (*measure*), /tʃ/ (*chair*), /dʒ/ (*jump*), /j/ (*yes*) |
| Back | Soft palate | /k/ (*cat*), /g/ (*go*), /ŋ/ (*sing*) |
| **No tongue involved** | Lips | /p/ (*pen*), /b/ (*bed*), /m/ (*map*), /w/ (*wet*) |
| **No tongue involved** | Lower lip + upper teeth | /f/ (*fan*), /v/ (*van*) |
| **No tongue involved** | Glottis (throat) | /h/ (*hat*) |

## 2. Voiced vs. Voiceless Sounds

Every sound is either **voiced** (vocal cords vibrate) or **voiceless** (vocal cords stay apart, only air passes through).

### How to Feel the Difference

Place the back of your hand against your throat and say these pairs slowly:

- **sss** (voiceless) → **zzz** (voiced) — feel the buzz appear for /z/
- **fff** (voiceless) → **vvv** (voiced) — feel the buzz appear for /v/

Alternatively, cover both ears with your hands. Voiced sounds create a hum you can hear from inside your head; voiceless sounds are silent internally.

## 3. Consonants — Voiced and Voiceless Pairs

Most English consonants come in **pairs** that share the same mouth position but differ only in voicing.

| Voiceless | Example | Voiced | Example | Tongue Zone | Target |
|-----------|---------|--------|---------|-------------|--------|
| /p/ | *pen* | /b/ | *bed* | — | Lips |
| /t/ | *ten* | /d/ | *den* | Tip | Gum ridge |
| /k/ | *cat* | /g/ | *got* | Back | Soft palate |
| /f/ | *fan* | /v/ | *van* | — | Lower lip + upper teeth |
| /θ/ | *think* | /ð/ | *this* | Tip | Teeth |
| /s/ | *sip* | /z/ | *zip* | Tip | Gum ridge |
| /ʃ/ | *shoe* | /ʒ/ | *measure* | Front (blade) | Hard palate |
| /tʃ/ | *chair* | /dʒ/ | *jump* | Front (blade) | Hard palate |
| /h/ | *hat* | — | — | None | — |

Some consonants have no voiceless counterpart — they are always voiced:

/m/, /n/, /ŋ/, /l/, /r/, /w/, /j/

## 4. Vowels — Always Voiced

All English vowels are **voiced**. There are no voiceless vowels in standard American English.

For vowels, **the tongue never contacts any surface** — it floats freely inside the mouth, shaping the resonance space by hovering at different heights and positions. For consonants, some articulator always makes contact or causes constriction (tongue, lips, or glottis). This is the key difference.

Vowels are shaped by:

1. **Tongue body position (front/back)** — how far forward or back the tongue body sits
2. **Tongue height** — how close the tongue is to the roof of the mouth; high = close to roof, low = far from roof
3. **Lip rounding** — whether the lips round (as in /u/) or spread (as in /i/)

| Tongue Body Position | Height | Lip Rounding | Sounds | Examples |
|----------------------|--------|-------------|--------|---------|
| Front | High | Unrounded | /i/, /ɪ/ | *eat*, *sit* |
| Front | Mid | Unrounded | /eɪ/, /ɛ/ | *face*, *bet* |
| Front | Low | Unrounded | /æ/ | *cat* |
| Central | Mid | Unrounded | /ə/, /ʌ/ | *about*, *cup* |
| Central | Mid (r-colored) | Unrounded | /ɝ/, /ɚ/ | *bird*, *butter* |
| Back | High | Rounded | /u/, /ʊ/ | *food*, *book* |
| Back | Mid | Rounded | /oʊ/, /ɔ/ | *home*, *law* |
| Back | Low | Unrounded | /ɑ/ | *father* |

Because all vowels involve continuous voicing and an open vocal tract, they carry the most volume and sustain the longest in connected speech.
